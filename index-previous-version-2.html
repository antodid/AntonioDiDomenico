About
“I learned that connection with people does not require a shared background — only a shared willingness to be present.”

I studied Economics and Business Economics at Utrecht University, finishing with a 7.85 GPA, a completed Honours Programme and a bachelor thesis graded 9.2. The degree taught me to think in models and data; the Honours Programme taught me to work closely with people who think nothing like I do — and that the second skill is worth at least as much as the first.

Then came Rio. A semester at PUC-Rio was the first time I had ever left Europe. I picked up Portuguese through everyday conversations rather than classrooms, learned samba because saying no felt like the wrong answer, and travelled across South America whenever the calendar allowed. I came back more independent, more curious, and much harder to unsettle.

These days I build things on the side — most recently Noce, a community map of overlooked places — while working part-time at Ex Primo, an Italian communication and marketing agency. In September 2026 I start the MSc in Finance and Investments at RSM Rotterdam. I learn fastest when I am in the middle of something I do not yet know how to finish.

Research

Do Chinese Capital Controls Raise the Cost of Debt for European Firms?

Bachelor Thesis · Utrecht University · Applied Economics Research Course · Graded 9.2 / 10

My thesis examines whether Chinese capital controls — the restrictions Beijing places on cross-border capital flows — make it more expensive for European companies to borrow money. The intuition is that when capital cannot move freely out of China, it affects global liquidity conditions in ways that ripple through to European credit markets. I tested this using panel data on 600 firms from the STOXX Europe 600 index across 22 years, combining econometric methods with a question I had become genuinely curious about through my interest in international finance. The result was a 9.2 — the grade I am most proud of, not because of the number but because the question was genuinely mine.
