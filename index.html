About

“I learned that connection with people does not require a shared background — only a shared willingness to be present.”

I studied Economics and Business Economics at Utrecht University, where I completed the Honours Programme alongside my degree and graduated with a 7.85 GPA. My bachelor thesis — an econometric study of whether Chinese capital controls raise borrowing costs for European firms — was graded a 9.2, and it taught me that the questions I care about most sit somewhere between data and the real world.

In my third year I spent a semester at PUC-Rio in Rio de Janeiro — the first time I had ever left Europe. I learned Portuguese not in a classroom but through everyday life: buying fruit at the market, asking for directions, dancing samba badly and being corrected kindly. Travelling through South America afterwards quietly changed my sense of what is far away and what is possible.

These days I build things on the side — most recently Noce, a map of the local knowledge that never makes it onto Google Maps — while working part-time at Ex Primo, an Italian communication and marketing agency. In September 2026 I start the MSc in Finance and Investments at RSM Rotterdam. I learn fastest in the middle of something I don't yet know how to finish.

Research

Do Chinese Capital Controls Raise the Cost of Debt for European Firms?

Bachelor Thesis · Utrecht University · Graded 9.2 / 10

My thesis examines whether Chinese capital controls — the restrictions Beijing places on cross-border capital flows — make it more expensive for European companies to borrow. When capital cannot move freely out of China, it shifts global liquidity in ways that ripple into European credit markets. I tested this on panel data of 600 STOXX Europe 600 firms across 22 years, pairing econometric methods with a question that was genuinely my own. The result was a 9.2 — the grade I'm proudest of, not for the number but because the question was mine.
