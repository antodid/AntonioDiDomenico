About
"I learn fastest when I am in the middle of something I do not yet know how to finish."

I studied Economics and Business Economics at Utrecht University, where I graduated with a GPA of 7.85 and a place in the Honours Programme — a selective track for students who want to go beyond the degree. My bachelor's thesis, graded 9.2, examined whether Chinese capital controls raise the cost of debt for European firms, drawing on panel data from the STOXX Europe 600 between 2002 and 2024. It taught me that the most interesting questions sit at the edge of two fields that don't usually talk to each other.

In August 2025 I moved to Rio de Janeiro for an exchange semester at PUC-Rio — the first time I had ever left Europe. I arrived not knowing Portuguese and left eight months later having made friends, navigated a city of eight million, and learned something that no course had taught me: that comfort is a limit, not a virtue. Brazil showed me how differently the same economic ideas land depending on the country you are standing in. I came back changed in ways I am still working out.

I am currently working part-time at Ex Primo, an Italian communication and marketing agency, while building Noce — a community map that collects the kind of local knowledge Google Maps will never have. In September 2026 I start an MSc in Finance and Investments at RSM Erasmus University in Rotterdam. I am also co-founding a small venture making decorative resin tiles with LED lighting, designing the first prototypes in Blender. The thread connecting all of it is the same: I like starting things I do not yet know how to finish.

Research

Bachelor Thesis — Graded 9.2

"Do Chinese Capital Controls Raise the Cost of Debt for European Firms? Evidence from the STOXX Europe 600, 2002–2024." An empirical study using fixed-effects panel regressions to test whether shifts in Chinese capital account openness transmit through global credit markets to European firm-level borrowing costs.
